# ZieSweets Company Profile — Project Overview & Design System

## 1. Project Overview

### Brand

**ZieSweets** is a bakery brand established in **2016**.

ZieSweets provides a variety of cakes and breads made with quality ingredients and a touch of care. With sweet flavors and soft textures, each creation is made to accompany both special moments and everyday occasions.

### Website Goal

Build a modern company profile website for ZieSweets that:

- Clearly introduces ZieSweets as a bakery brand.
- Presents products through strong visual storytelling.
- Builds a recognizable and trustworthy brand presence.
- Makes the existing ZieSweets identity feel more modern without losing its character.
- Provides clear paths to discover products and contact/order ZieSweets.
- Feels like a professionally art-directed brand website rather than a generic template.

The website is primarily a **brand/company profile**, not an e-commerce application.

---

# 2. Design Direction

## Overall Direction

The visual direction should be inspired by the provided reference website layout:

- Clean white/cream canvas.
- Large editorial hero composition.
- Large product photography as the visual focal point.
- Strong, expressive typography.
- Short, confident copy.
- Clear CTA.
- Spacious layout.
- Small navigation/header.
- Subtle visual details rather than excessive decoration.

However, **do not copy the reference website directly**.

Use the reference as inspiration for its:

- Composition
- Visual hierarchy
- Product-first presentation
- Editorial typography
- Spacious layout
- Brand-focused storytelling

The final design must be distinctly **ZieSweets**.

---

## Brand Identity

The existing ZieSweets logo is the primary brand reference.

Current visual identity:

- Warm yellow/gold
- Deep chocolate brown
- Warm cream/off-white
- Decorative/script-style logo mark
- Friendly confectionery character

The website should feel like:

> **A modern editorial bakery brand with a warm, handcrafted character.**

Desired characteristics:

- Warm
- Appetizing
- Premium
- Friendly
- Confident
- Handcrafted
- Slightly nostalgic
- Modern

Avoid making it overly childish or overly luxurious.

# 3. Color System

Start from the existing ZieSweets identity instead of inventing an unrelated palette.

### Primary

**ZieSweets Yellow / Gold**

Use as an accent and brand highlight.

Possible applications:

- CTA emphasis
- Small decorative elements
- Active states
- Product labels
- Background blocks
- Section accents

### Secondary

**Chocolate Brown**

Use for:

- Primary typography
- Navigation
- Buttons
- Borders
- Brand details

### Neutral

**Warm Cream / Off-White**

Use for:

- Main page background
- Section backgrounds
- Content areas

White may also be used where needed for visual breathing room.

### Important

Do not make the entire website yellow.

Yellow should function as a recognizable brand accent while cream, white, brown, and product photography create visual balance.

Avoid:

- Purple gradients
- Blue SaaS palettes
- Neon gradients
- Generic pastel bakery palettes
- Excessive glassmorphism

# 4. Homepage Direction

The homepage should feel like a continuous brand story rather than a collection of generic website sections.

Suggested structure:

## 4.1 Header

Minimal and clean.

Possible structure:

```text
ZIE SWEETS                         HOME   ABOUT   PRODUCTS   CONTACT
```

Requirements:

- Logo on the left.
- Navigation on the right.
- Strong but minimal typography.
- Sticky navigation may be used if it improves usability.
- Mobile navigation should be intentionally designed rather than simply collapsed.

Do not overcrowd the header.

---

## 4.2 Hero

The hero is the most important visual section.

Use the provided real ZieSweets product photography.

Preferred composition:

- Strong headline on one side.
- Large product image on the other.
- Large whitespace.
- Small supporting copy.
- Clear CTA.
- Optional small decorative brand element.

The hero should resemble the **editorial product-focused composition** of the provided reference.

Example content direction:

> **Sweet moments, made with love.**

Supporting copy can communicate that ZieSweets has been creating cakes and breads since 2016.

CTA examples:

- Explore Our Products
- Discover ZieSweets
- View Our Creations

Do not use generic marketing copy such as:

> "We create unforgettable experiences for everyone."

Copy should remain specific to ZieSweets.

---

## 4.3 Brand Introduction

Introduce ZieSweets naturally after the hero.

Possible content:

> **Since 2016, ZieSweets has been creating cakes and breads made with quality ingredients and a touch of love.**

Use the actual brand description:

> ZieSweets berdiri sejak tahun 2016 dan bergerak di bidang bakery. Kami menghadirkan berbagai pilihan kue dan roti yang dibuat dengan bahan berkualitas dan sentuhan cinta. Dengan cita rasa manis dan tekstur yang lembut, setiap kreasi kami dibuat untuk menemani momen spesial dan keseharianmu.

Do not make this section overly text-heavy.

Combine typography with imagery or an editorial composition.

---

## 4.4 Product Showcase

Products should be presented visually.

Do NOT automatically use a standard grid of identical cards.

Prefer:

- Editorial product compositions
- Large featured product
- Asymmetric image layouts
- Different image sizes
- Product categories
- Horizontal visual sections
- Carefully designed image crops

The product photography should be the main visual element.

If actual product images are available, always prefer them over generated imagery or stock imagery.

---

## 4.5 Brand Story / About

Explain the brand in a concise and visually engaging way.

Focus on:

- Established in 2016
- Bakery identity
- Quality ingredients
- Soft textures
- Sweet flavors
- Creating products for everyday moments and special occasions

Do not invent:

- Awards
- Number of stores
- Customer statistics
- Claims about popularity
- Certifications
- Production numbers
- Company history that has not been provided

---

## 4.6 Closing CTA

End the page with a strong but simple CTA.

The section should feel like a continuation of the brand rather than a generic SaaS CTA.

Possible direction:

> **Make today a little sweeter.**

Then provide a clear path to:

- Contact ZieSweets
- Order / inquire
- Instagram
- WhatsApp

Only use contact information that is actually provided by the project assets/content.

---

# 5. Typography

Typography should be a major part of the visual identity.

Use a strong display typeface for:

- Hero headlines
- Section titles
- Large editorial statements

Use a highly readable supporting typeface for:

- Body text
- Navigation
- Metadata
- Buttons

Typography should create contrast without becoming chaotic.

Avoid using too many fonts.

Recommended maximum:

- 1 display family
- 1 supporting/body family

If the existing brand typography can be preserved or adapted appropriately, prioritize brand consistency.

---

# 6. Photography

Photography is a core part of the design.

The provided ZieSweets product photos should be treated as first-class visual assets.

Image treatment should be:

- Large
- Clean
- High quality
- Appetizing
- Carefully cropped
- Properly composed

Do not cover product photography with unnecessary UI.

Do not generate fake product imagery when actual images are available.

Do not use random stock bakery photography simply to fill empty sections.

If an image is missing, create a clean content placeholder or leave the section intentionally simple rather than inventing fake assets.

---

# 7. Motion & Interaction

Motion should support the brand experience.

Use subtle:

- Fade/reveal transitions
- Image reveals
- Typography transitions
- Hover states
- Smooth section transitions
- Small image movement where appropriate

Motion should feel intentional and premium.

Avoid:

- Excessive parallax
- Constant floating elements
- Bouncing objects
- Animation on every component
- Long page-loading animations
- Overly dramatic transitions
- Scroll-jacking

The design should still look good with animations disabled.

---

# 8. Responsive Design

Design deliberately for:

- Desktop
- Laptop
- Tablet
- Mobile

Do not simply scale down the desktop layout.

Mobile should have its own composition.

Pay particular attention to:

- Hero image placement
- Headline size
- Navigation
- CTA placement
- Image cropping
- Section spacing
- Touch targets
- Horizontal overflow

No accidental horizontal scrolling.

---

# 9. Coding Principles

The codebase must be **clean, reusable, maintainable, and production-oriented**.

## 9.1 SOLID

Apply SOLID principles where they make sense.

Especially:

- Single Responsibility
- Open/Closed
- Dependency Inversion where appropriate

Do not force theoretical abstractions into simple UI components.

The goal is practical maintainability, not architecture for architecture's sake.

---

## 9.2 Reusability

Build reusable components for repeated UI patterns.

Examples:

- Header
- Navigation
- Button
- Section wrapper
- Section heading
- Product presentation
- Image container
- Footer
- CTA
- Typography primitives where useful

Do not duplicate the same markup across multiple sections when a reusable component makes sense.

However, **do not over-abstract unique sections** just to make every file reusable.

Use abstraction when it improves clarity and maintenance.

---

## 9.3 No Spaghetti Code

Avoid:

- Huge components
- Deeply nested conditional rendering
- Repeated markup
- Hardcoded duplicated values
- Inline logic that belongs elsewhere
- Unclear naming
- Components that do five unrelated things
- Global hacks
- CSS overrides fighting other CSS overrides

A component should have a clear responsibility.

If a component becomes unnecessarily large, split it into meaningful pieces.

Do not split components purely based on line count.

---

# 10. Data & Content

Separate content/data from presentation when practical.

For example, product information should not require rewriting a large JSX/TSX structure.

Prefer structured data:

```ts
const products = [
  {
    name: "...",
    category: "...",
    image: "...",
    description: "...",
  },
];
```

Then render reusable presentation components.

Do not create an unnecessarily complex CMS/data layer for a simple company profile.

Keep the implementation proportional to the project.

---

# 11. Styling Rules

Keep styling predictable and centralized.

Prefer:

- Design tokens
- CSS variables / theme values
- Reusable utility classes
- Component-level styles where appropriate
- Consistent spacing scale
- Consistent typography scale

Avoid:

- Random one-off values everywhere
- Duplicate styles
- Excessive arbitrary pixel values
- `!important` unless genuinely necessary
- CSS hacks
- Inline styles for things that should be reusable

If Tailwind is already used in the project, use it consistently instead of introducing another styling system.

---

# 12. Component Architecture

Use a clear hierarchy.

Example:

```text
src/
├── components/
│   ├── layout/
│   │   ├── Header
│   │   └── Footer
│   │
│   ├── ui/
│   │   ├── Button
│   │   ├── SectionHeading
│   │   └── Image
│   │
│   └── sections/
│       ├── Hero
│       ├── About
│       ├── Products
│       └── Contact
│
├── data/
│   └── products
│
├── pages/
│   └── Home
│
└── ...
```

Adapt this structure to the existing repository.

Do not blindly restructure the project if a suitable architecture already exists.

---

# 13. Accessibility

The website must be accessible.

Ensure:

- Semantic HTML
- Proper heading hierarchy
- Descriptive image alt text
- Keyboard-accessible navigation
- Visible focus states
- Sufficient contrast
- Buttons are actual buttons
- Links are actual links
- Images do not contain critical information without text alternatives

Do not sacrifice accessibility for visual effects.

---

# 14. Performance

Keep the website lightweight.

Prioritize:

- Optimized images
- Appropriate image dimensions
- Lazy loading where appropriate
- Minimal dependencies
- Avoiding unnecessary JavaScript
- Avoiding expensive animations
- Reusing components

Do not install a library for something that can be implemented simply with existing project capabilities.

---

# 15. Content Rules

Only use information that is actually available.

Known brand information:

- ZieSweets was established in 2016.
- ZieSweets operates in the bakery sector.
- ZieSweets offers cakes and breads.
- Products use quality ingredients.
- The brand emphasizes sweetness, soft textures, and care.
- Products are intended for special moments and everyday occasions.

Do not fabricate business facts.

If content is missing, use a clearly marked placeholder or ask for the information.

---

# 16. AI Slop Prevention

This is a strict requirement.

Before considering a section finished, ask:

### Does this section exist because the brand needs it?

If not, remove it.

### Does this look like a generic AI landing page?

If yes, redesign it.

### Is the product imagery doing enough visual work?

If not, improve the composition.

### Are there too many cards?

Reduce them.

### Are there too many rounded containers?

Reduce them.

### Are there too many animations?

Reduce them.

### Is the page visually noisy?

Simplify it.

### Could this design belong to any random bakery?

If yes, make the visual language more ZieSweets-specific.

---

# 17. Development Workflow

Before modifying code:

1. Inspect the existing repository.
2. Identify the framework and current architecture.
3. Inspect existing assets.
4. Identify the actual ZieSweets logo and product images.
5. Inspect existing dependencies.
6. Identify existing reusable components.
7. Do not modify anything yet.

Then provide:

### Repository Findings

- Existing architecture
- Relevant files
- Existing assets
- Existing components
- Existing styling approach
- Existing dependencies

### Proposed Design

Explain:

- Homepage structure
- Visual direction
- Typography
- Color system
- Product image treatment
- Motion strategy
- Responsive strategy

Only after this analysis should implementation begin.

---

# 18. Implementation Rules

When implementation starts:

- Make focused changes.
- Reuse existing code where appropriate.
- Do not rewrite unrelated files.
- Do not regenerate the entire application.
- Do not create duplicate components.
- Do not add dependencies without a clear reason.
- Do not create fake assets.
- Do not generate fake company information.
- Do not run unnecessary build commands.
- Verify each meaningful milestone before continuing.

Prioritize **quality over speed**.

---

# 19. Definition of Done

The ZieSweets website is considered complete when:

- The brand identity is immediately recognizable.
- The existing logo is used correctly.
- Product photography is visually prominent.
- The website feels modern without becoming generic.
- The homepage has a clear visual story.
- Desktop and mobile layouts both feel intentionally designed.
- Components are reusable without excessive abstraction.
- Code is clean and maintainable.
- There is no duplicated spaghetti markup.
- Styling is consistent.
- Animations are restrained.
- Accessibility basics are implemented.
- No unsupported/fabricated business claims are present.
- The result feels like a real bakery brand website designed by a professional team.

---

**Do not start coding immediately.**

First:

1. Audit the repository.
2. Inspect all available ZieSweets assets.
3. Identify the existing logo and product photography.

5. Propose the homepage structure.
6. Propose the visual system.
